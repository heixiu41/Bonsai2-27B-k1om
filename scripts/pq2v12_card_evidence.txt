CONCLUSION: vpermd, vpsrld, vpaddd, vpandd, vpord, vfmadd, vmulps all measure
the SAME throughput, and a memory operand costs nothing extra.  H1 is
supported, H2 is refuted.  On this part there is no "cheap op class": the only
currency is the NUMBER of 512-bit vector operations.

From `icc -mmic -O3 -S pq2v16.s`, grouped by the source line that produced them

V1 (shipped, pq2_0_dequant_block_imci inlined + 8 FMA, ICC unrolled the j loop
by 2; the line-80 block below is therefore TWO blocks):
  line 80: vpermd x32, vpsrlvd x16, vmulps x16, vloadunpackld x4,
           vloadunpackhd x4, + scalar
  lines 81-88: vfmadd132ps x4, vfmadd231ps x4, vmovaps x4, vmulps x4
  => per 128 weights: 16 vpermd + 8 vpsrlvd + 8 vmulps + 8 vfmadd + 4 vmovaps
     + 4 unpack-load instrs (+ 8 stores + 8 x loads which are folded into the
     FMAs or the store)   == the previous agent's count (16 vpermd / 8 srlv /
     8 mulps / 8 fmadd), reproduced here.

V10c (pq2v16.s lines 106-133):
  8 vpermd, 6 vpsrld, 8 vmulps, 8 vfmadd, 4 vmovaps, 4 unpack-load instrs,
  2 vmovaps for the LOAD16 staging  => 40 in-loop instructions per 128 weights.

V12 inline (lines 139-162): 8 vpermd, 2 vpsrld, 2 vmulps, 8 vfmadd,
  4+2 vmovaps, 4 unpack  => 30 in-loop instructions per 128 weights.

V12 helper (line 205, the whole inlined block body on one source line):
  vpermd x8, vfmadd132ps x8, vmovaps x6, vloadunpackld x2, vloadunpackhd x2,
  vpsrld x2, vmulps x2, vprefetch1 x1  => 31 in-loop instructions per 128 weights,
  i.e. the same instruction mix as V12 inline but WITH a compiler-inserted
  software prefetch (vprefetch1), which is what the explicit pf8..pf32 variants
  confirm is worth ~10-13% of the 19% helper advantage.

So: V12 removes 10 of V10c's 40 in-loop instructions (-25%) and adds one
prefetch, and measures 1.55-1.56x vs V1 where V10c measures 1.12-1.15x.

SECTION 10 -- WHAT DID NOT WORK (negative results, all measured)
================================================================================
1. Replacing the LUT permute with integer arithmetic (V3, V7/V8/V9, and the
   IEEE-754 "OR 0x4B000000 then subtract 2^23" bias idea).
   pq2prim/pq2prim2/pq2prim3: a vpermd costs EXACTLY the same as a vpaddd, and
   pq2v13 Q2: in the real kernel, replacing all 8 vpermd by 8 vpaddd changes
   nothing (1228.6 vs 1229.5 M w/s).  Every such scheme needs >= 2 ops to
   replace 1, so it can only lose.  The bias-float route also needs a per-lane
   subtraction of 2^23 * x to avoid catastrophic cancellation (measured in the
   doc's V3: 452 M w/s, 2.1x slower, and numerically wrong), so it is dead for
   two independent reasons.
2. Scaling the x operand instead of the LUT (pq2v14 V14c): 0.964x -- 8
   multiplies on the x side cost more than 2 on the LUT side.
3. Putting K>1 blocks in flight per iteration (pq2v15, same binary, same run):
   K=2 1316, K=3 1362, K=4 1374, K=5 1389, K=8 1427 vs K=1 1466 M w/s.
   Manual multi-block unrolling HURTS; register pressure and schedule length
   outweigh the extra ILP.
4. Explicit software prefetch is not a full substitute for the scheduling
   difference: pf8/pf16/pf32 reach 1.42-1.47x where the helper form reaches
   1.55-1.56x.  pf4 is worse than no prefetch at all (1.11-1.15x).
5. The 8 broadcast permutes of the V1 decoder are NOT worth attacking by
   instruction substitution: V10 already removed them by regrouping the dot
   product by 2-bit field, which is the right way (it also removes the need for
   a byte-wide broadcast, which the hardware cannot do at all -- see the
   previous agent's exhaustive proof that 126 of 128 (half,group,lane) triples
   admit no correct lane-local shift).
